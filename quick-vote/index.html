Quick Vote

Only the people you let in can vote.

Read out the PIN and people join from their phones with a name. Each name waits until the host lets it in, so only people with the right to vote can vote. Results stay hidden until the host turns them on.

Made for a church fellowship's committee election, and fine for any room where only some people should vote.

Six digits and a name.

Voters open the site and type the PIN the host reads out. Nobody signs up, and nothing gets installed.

The phone is live. Tap around.

  • The cursor moves on by itself. After the sixth digit it jumps to the name field, so a room full of people can join fast.
  • The host lets people in. Each name waits in a queue until the host approves it, one at a time or all at once.
  • Any six digits work here. This phone is a demo: type a PIN and a name, then tap Join.

One tap, then submit.

Options are big rows you can hit with a thumb. Nothing counts until Submit, so a wrong tap is easy to fix.

  • Single or multiple choice. The host decides when making the poll, and the ballot says which.
  • Anonymous if you want it. With Anonymous on, the host sees counts but not who picked what.

Bars that move as votes land.

Results stay hidden until the host turns Live on. Then every screen in the room shows the same bars at once, and they keep moving as votes come in.

  • The leader is green. Every other option is grey, so the answer reads from across a room.
  • Your pick is marked. A small check sits next to the option you chose.

The host runs the room.

The host's screen has the PIN to read out, the queue of people waiting, and the switch that shows results.

  • Approve people as they arrive. Or approve everyone in the queue with one tap.
  • Live is one switch. Off, voters see only that their vote counted. On, they see the bars.

Details

  • No accounts and nothing to install.
  • Single or multiple choice, with names shown to the host or fully anonymous.
  • Polls take 2 to 20 options.
  • Results show as bars that update as each vote lands, with the leading option in green.
  • Load-tested with 300 people voting at the same moment.
